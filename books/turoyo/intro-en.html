Introduction. Turoyo and its people

Whose language is it

Turoyo is the mother tongue of the Suryoye (Assyrians-Syriacs) of Tur Abdin, a hilly region in south-eastern Turkey between the towns of Mardin, Midyat and Nusaybin (ancient Nisibis). Speakers call their language Surayt and themselves Suryoye. The name Turoyo means "of the mountain", that is, "the language of Tur Abdin". Reference works list it as Turoyo (ISO 639-3: tru), a Central Neo-Aramaic language.

Aramaic through the centuries

Turoyo continues the history of Aramaic, which almost three thousand years ago became the common language of the Assyrian Empire and later of the whole Near East. Its written form, Classical Syriac, took shape in Edessa (Urhoy) and became the language of Christian literature. From the 4th century Tur Abdin was one of the main centres of the West Syriac church, with ancient monasteries such as Mor Gabriel (397).

Classical Syriac remained the language of books and worship, while the villages of Tur Abdin kept a spoken language from which Turoyo grew. It preserves many old Aramaic words and has borrowed from Kurdish, Arabic and Turkish.

Diaspora and writing

In the 20th century the Suryoye lived through the genocide of 1914–1915 (Sayfo) and later emigrated in large numbers. Today most Turoyo speakers live in Europe, above all in Sweden and Germany, and also in the Netherlands, Switzerland, the United States and other countries.

For a long time Turoyo was mainly spoken. It is written in the western Serto script, usually in an etymological spelling like Classical Syriac. In Sweden a Latin spelling of Turoyo was also created for school textbooks. In this book every word is given in Serto with a reading in Latin letters.

How to use this book

The book follows the CEFR levels from A1 to C2. Each level has lessons with explanations, words, examples and reading texts, followed by exercises. The answers and a dictionary are at the end of the book.
